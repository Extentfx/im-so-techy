I. The Estate

Two centuries of quiet,
kept by hand.

The Alotion family took these riverbanks in 1897 with a single intention — to keep them. The limestone clubhouse was raised from stone hauled up the White River itself, and the first eighteen holes were laid through oak grove by grove over the following decade.

The estate has never changed hands, and never changed spirit. What the founders set down — long shadows, slow mornings, an iron gate that opens only for members — is what remains. The Club receives its members as its grandfathers did: quietly, and for good.

II. The Course

An eighteen-hole walk
through the Ozarks.

The course follows the river the way a story follows a road — one good turn after another. Edmund Alotion, the Club's first chairman and its only architect, drew the holes in pencil over a single long lunch, and the ground has kept his lines since.

III. Membership

Membership is extended,
not sold.

The Club admits no more than twelve new members each season. Admission is by the introduction of two existing members, or by the invitation of the Membership Committee. We would rather you arrived knowing someone here.
